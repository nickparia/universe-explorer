# LOOP — the game of Solace

The source of truth for what the traveler *does* and why they return.
Companion to `SOL.md` (who the ship is). When code and this file
disagree, this file wins; when this file and the vibe disagree, the
vibe wins. Working name for the direction: **Stardew's loop, Solace's
soul.**

## The one-line loop

> Read the roster → fly an expedition → survey the ground and place
> equipment well → your outposts work while you're away → refine,
> research, and build the ship room by room → deeper places need what
> you've learned → return to expand, never to repeat.

Effort at every step. Every payback caused by the player. Everything
built is visible. Nothing built is ever lost.

## Pillars (laws, in order of precedence)

1. **The vibe is sovereign.** Soothing and awe-inspiring. No combat
   (environment is the only adversary). No death. No energy bars, no
   hard resets, no score numerals. Diegetic everything — see
   [[solace-no-fourth-wall]].
2. **Effort → payback.** A reward for doing nothing has no value. All
   progression is caused by player action. Time alone never gifts;
   time only *completes what the player commissioned*.
3. **Visible builds.** A build you can't see is a spreadsheet. Every
   built thing has a place the camera can be: the hull in third
   person, rooms as interior camera stations, outposts on real ground.
4. **Home sacred, run riskable.** The ship, rooms, and outposts are
   never at stake. Only the current expedition's margin is: cargo,
   time, consumables, a drone left behind. Failure = withdrawal, and
   withdrawal is atmosphere (the quiet limp home), not punishment.
5. **Real places, hand-signed.** No procedural filler. Groundside
   terrain is built from real NASA elevation data, then hand-lit and
   hand-composed to the same sign-off standard as the orbital
   locations. Sites, not globes.
6. **Solve once, own forever.** The challenge of a place is solved by
   understanding it — then infrastructure makes the solution
   permanent. The player is never asked to repeat a slog; they are
   invited back to expand.
7. **Tasks are the heartbeat.** The seed player's words: "I never
   cared about the interactions… I just like the tasks." The duty
   roster is a first-class organ; charm budget concentrates on Sol,
   the sky, and the ground — not on a cast.

## The ship — the farm

The ship gets a body — the SOLACE, built in the school of the
Nostromo (industrial refinery-tug massing, gantry spine, practicals,
stencils; the school, never the trademark). Two ways to see it:

- **Exterior (third person)**: orbit the camera around your own
  vessel. Everything built accretes visibly on the hull. The
  silhouette of your ship is your save file.
- **Interiors (FULL 3D, walkable)** *(amended 2026-08-04 — the
  composed-camera-station plan is dead: "if we make it just camera
  scenes, we are just like the game Myst")*: the interior is a real
  first-person environment, walked with the same boots as the ground.
  It rides the ship's frame inside the live scene, so every window is
  an honest window — sit in the observatory while the landmark you
  are orbiting wheels past the glass. The ground engine is the
  precedent: if a planet can be walked, so can a home. Rooms are
  built one compartment at a time, the observatory first (it IS the
  vibe), then the cryo bay (the wake sequence gains its set), the
  mess, the bridge.

### Rooms (initial set)

| Room | Function | Loop role |
|---|---|---|
| Bridge | the current cockpit/terminal, Sol's chamber | exists today |
| Workshop | refining, drone assembly, gear crafting | converts hauls → components |
| Garden dome | real-day crops from exotic flora | the daily-cadence organ |
| Gallery / museum | specimen collection, one slot per find | the long collection pull |
| Observatory | research bench; points at what you've charted | recipes / tech |
| Cinema | replays voyages, postcard slideshows | memory as furniture |
| Quiet room | no function; earned from the Boötes crossing | the point of it all |

Rooms need: **research** (Observatory time + observations you flew
for) + **components** (refined materials) + **build time** (real
hours; drones assemble it on the hull, watchably; you return to a
finished wing — completing *your* order, per Pillar 2).

## Groundside — sites, not globes

Full 3D, bounded, authored. Each world gets 1–3 hand-composed landing
sites: real navigable terrain with dynamic sun, built from real DEMs
(HiRISE/MOLA for Mars, LROC for the Moon), walkable and rovable, at
the fidelity bar of the orbital locations.

**Challenge on the ground, three layers (no enemy anywhere):**

- **Traversal** — the terrain resists: crevasse fields, sliding
  scree, low-g momentum, lava tubes lit only by lights you place
  (which persist — your breadcrumbs on the world).
- **Windows** — weather and orbital mechanics as the clock: the
  moving shadow line on Mercury, Io's radiation minutes, Mars dust
  storms with readable warning signs, the geyser's schedule.
- **Preparation** — the Subnautica law: depth gated by gear, gear by
  materials, materials by depth one notch shallower. Ground challenge
  feeds back into the ship loop.

## The survey law — ask before you build

The FIRST act on any world is planting a survey stake. A stake answers
the ground it stands in: slope, real sun-hours against the real
horizon, mineral signature, atmosphere — and its circle of VERIFIED
ground appears on the charts. The orbital map always shows everything
(no fog of war — the ship chose this site from orbit), but only
surveyed ground is *known*: readings, hazards and resources live
inside the circles. Exploration is converting orbital estimate into
ground truth, circle by circle — and each world varies what makes
that hard (a toxic atmosphere is answered in the lab; a radiation
window is answered with timing; distance is answered with the rover).
Information is the gate: what a stake reports decides what you can —
and must — build next.

## The ten-times law — chores become blueprints

Every manual act is counted. Around the tenth repetition of anything
(stakes planted, loads hauled, cores drilled), the ship does NOT do it
for you — it makes the automation *researchable*: the survey drone
blueprint unlocks because you have earned the right to stop doing
surveys by hand, and you still have to build the drone. The chore's
ending is itself a thing you construct. The lineage scales: hand
stake → site survey drone → orbital survey pass → drones dispatched
to worlds you have never landed on. Automation is the reward for
repetition, never a substitute for the first ten times.

## Outposts — solve once, own forever (the keystone)

The quest of a site is to **establish and grow refining
infrastructure**, and the challenge is **placement**:

- A survey pass reads the land: ore veins, solar exposure, storm
  shelter, slope stability, dust, line-of-sight for relay. All of it
  is legible in the real terrain — placement skill IS terrain
  literacy.
- A well-placed extractor/refinery runs while you're away (Pillar 2:
  it completes *your* solution; it never magics up anything you
  didn't site, power, and connect).
- Poor placement isn't destroyed — it underperforms, silts up, needs
  visits. The land teaches; you re-site with what you've learned.
- **Return is expansion**: new research unlocks deeper extraction at
  a site you know (the thermal bore for the deposit you surveyed but
  couldn't touch), a second pad, a relay to a harder site. You come
  back because you *can do more now*, never to redo anything.
- Outposts are visible: their lights from orbit on the night side of
  Mars; their slow work when you land. Presence made permanent.

Drones divide the labor: **orbit-gatherables** (ring ice, belt metals,
comet volatiles) are droned from the ship, watchably; **special
materials and the awe** live at crafted sites, reached on foot.

## Stakes

- Fuel closes the economy: refined from harvested volatiles; makes
  distance real and provisioning a pleasure. Run dry → adrift → Sol
  manages the limp home. Atmosphere, not game-over.
- Hull/suit condition: heat, radiation, tide. Costs repair materials
  and time. Never destruction.
- The passive layer stays free: drift mode and cruises never touch a
  gauge; Sol stewards reserves invisibly. Two audiences, one ship.

## The duty roster — the heartbeat

A quiet list in the terminal's amber, composed each session from real
state, braiding three cadences (always one thing finishable now, one
thing worth returning for):

- *minutes*: water the spore garden · unload drone two
- *days*: harvest the ice-blooms · research completes tomorrow ·
  drone due back from the rings
- *seasons*: perihelion in three days · the museum lacks a Europa
  core · the Valles outpost can take a second extractor now

Sol reads the roster the way a first officer would — it is a report,
not a quest log.

## The garden — real-day crops

Exotic flora grown from expedition finds: nebula spores, comet
ice-blooms, something that germinates only in magnetar light. Real
days (the Animal Crossing clock): the crop that needs three evenings
of tending is an appointment. Yields feed recipes (growth mediums,
suit linings, Sol's quiet fondnesses).

## The calendar — festivals in the sky

Dated, recurring, plannable: meteor showers, conjunctions, eclipses,
a comet's perihelion. The event is the occasion, not the reward — the
harvest flown during it is the game. (Rare windows gate rare
materials: perihelion volatiles, shower dust.)

## The museum

One slot per specimen per place; visible empty spaces do the pull.
Cheap to build, endless to finish.

## Sol

Already built (bond, seasons, memory) and woven through: reads the
roster, appraises placements the way it appraised nothing before
("the southern shelf would have seen more sun — but this will
serve"), remembers your first outpost, asks — rarely — to see one.

rover (belt iron + ring ice) → Mars survey kit (rover + silica) →

+ Io sulfur, needs radiation window) → Europa deep descent (bore +
thermal suit) → …

## Phasing

- **Phase 0 — Mars, the feel** *(next)*: one real-DEM Mars site in
  full 3D; land, walk, rove; light and dust and sound signed off by
  eye — mechanics and mood first, exactly as Sol was built before the
  systems were. No loop yet.
- **Phase 1 — the survey and the first outpost**: terrain reading,
  placement, one extractor that persists and works.
- **Phase 2 — the ship's body**: exterior view, workshop, refining;
  hauls become components.
- **Phase 3 — rooms + roster + garden**: the daily braid.
- **Phase 4 — calendar, museum, deeper chains, more worlds.**

Deferred, deliberately: combat (revisit only with evidence the loop
needs it), the radio-voice cast (garnish, later), multiplayer
presence. (Walkable interiors were deferred here once — un-deferred
2026-08-04; the ship is the home, and homes are walked.)
